Sheet 03 — About

Adeyemi
Adebanjo

Engineer by training, project manager by practice and now by qualification, and the person the control room called when a feeder went dark.

I started as a graduate engineer on consulting projects in Lagos, doing feasibility studies and cost estimates, and learned early that a project is decided long before anyone turns a spanner.

Then came five years of project administration at Lahor Limited — the documentation, the registers, the minutes, the status reports. It is the part of delivery nobody puts on a poster, and it is where I learned that a project you cannot evidence is a project you cannot defend.

The years that shaped me were the eleven at Ibadan Electricity Distribution Company. A distribution network is not a project that finishes — it is an asset you keep alive while you rebuild parts of it, and the discipline that job teaches is holding three clocks at once: the maintenance schedule, the restoration clock during a fault, and the delivery schedule of whatever you are upgrading that quarter.

It also taught me that the technical problem is rarely the hard one. The hard one is the sponsor, the regulator, the contractor, the crew and the customer all needing a different account of the same outage, at the same time, and all of them entitled to it.

What I care about is the unglamorous part. Assurance checkpoints before handover, not defect lists afterwards. A RAID log that somebody actually reads. An incident report that changes the maintenance plan instead of filling a folder. Cutting delivery defects by 45% was not a clever idea; it was inspection gates applied consistently until they became normal.

I moved to Sheffield for an MSc in Project Management at Sheffield Hallam, completed in 2026. My dissertation examined data-driven risk management among electricity distribution companies — the same problem I spent a decade solving by instinct, studied properly. I am now working toward project delivery, operations and service management roles in UK utilities, infrastructure and IT-enabled change.

I hold PRINCE2 and Agile certification, I am a registered engineer with COREN and a corporate member of the Nigerian Society of Engineers, and I take a permit to work seriously enough to be unpopular about it.
